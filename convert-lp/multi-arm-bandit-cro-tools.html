Common questions about multi-arm bandit testing

A standard A/B test splits traffic evenly between variants for the full duration of the test, then declares a winner at the end. A multi-arm bandit test dynamically reallocates traffic toward better-performing variants as data accumulates, so the winning variant receives more traffic while the test is still running.

The practical difference: with a standard A/B test, you pay a fixed "cost of exploration" for the entire test duration. With a multi-arm bandit, that cost decreases over time as the algorithm exploits what it's learning. For high-traffic pages and time-sensitive campaigns, this can represent meaningful revenue recovered during the experiment itself.

Multi-arm bandits are most valuable when the cost of running a losing variant is high relative to the benefit of a statistically clean result. That typically means high-traffic pages, time-bounded campaigns (like seasonal promotions), and situations where you need to act quickly rather than wait for a traditional significance threshold.

Standard A/B tests are still preferable when you need a clean causal inference, when your traffic is lower and the algorithm has less signal to work with, or when your organization needs a methodologically conservative result for regulatory or compliance reasons. In many programs, both approaches run concurrently on different test types.

Convert supports both. The multi-arm bandit allocation engine typically pairs with Bayesian probability, which is well-suited for continuous traffic reallocation because it produces a posterior probability estimate that updates naturally as new data arrives. You can set a winner threshold (for example, 95% probability of being best) and Convert will promote or reallocate accordingly.

If your testing program also requires Frequentist validation for long-term learnings or compliance documentation, that engine is available in the same platform. You're not forced to choose one model for your entire program.

There is no hard cutoff, but multi-arm bandits perform best when there's enough signal for the algorithm to make meaningful reallocation decisions. In practice, sites with fewer than 5,000 monthly unique visitors on the tested page will see limited benefit, because the algorithm needs sufficient data to distinguish real performance differences from random variance.

As a general guideline, if your current A/B tests take months to reach significance, a multi-arm bandit will also take time to converge. The allocation advantage is most pronounced in the 20,000+ monthly visitors range. Convert's support team can help you assess whether a given test configuration makes sense for your traffic level before you start.

Sequential testing addresses the "peeking problem": if you monitor a standard A/B test continuously and make decisions based on early results, you inflate your false positive rate. Multi-arm bandits require continuous monitoring by design, so pairing them with sequential testing controls lets you defend early reallocation decisions without inflating Type I errors.

In Convert, you configure your statistical method and threshold at the experiment level. For bandit experiments where you anticipate making interim decisions, setting the sequential testing control before the test launches ensures each reallocation point is evaluated against a statistically valid boundary rather than an arbitrary peek.

Once a winning variant meets your configured probability threshold, Convert can automatically shift 100% of traffic to that variant while you prepare the permanent implementation. For front-end experiments, you can extend the experiment to serve only the winner indefinitely. For full-stack experiments, Convert's feature flag integration lets you promote the winning variant to a full rollout instantly, without a new engineering deployment.

The feature flag layer also gives you a kill switch. If the winning variant produces unexpected downstream effects post-rollout, a common risk with adaptive algorithms that optimize for a narrow conversion metric, you can revert to the control immediately from the Convert dashboard without a code push.
